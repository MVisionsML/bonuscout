## What Is 10% Cashback?

A 10% cashback bonus returns 10% of your **net losses** over a defined period — typically weekly or monthly — as withdrawable cash, usually with very low or zero wagering requirements.

The math in plain terms: if you deposit €500 and finish the week with €100 remaining (net loss of €400), 10% cashback returns €40 to your account. That €40 is then either immediately withdrawable (true 0x wagering) or requires a single re-bet (1x wagering) before withdrawal.

This is structurally different from a welcome bonus. A welcome bonus front-loads value but requires 30–50x wagering to extract — meaning most welcome bonuses statistically convert to less than their face value once the wagering math runs. Cashback works the opposite way: it's paid against actual losses you've already taken, so the entire amount is real money rather than play-through obligation.

## Weekly vs Monthly: The Variance Math

This is where most "10% cashback" comparisons fall apart in practice. Two casinos can both advertise "10% cashback" with one paying weekly and one paying monthly, and the weekly version produces substantially more real money for the same player behavior. Here's why:

**The variance effect** — slot play produces winning weeks and losing weeks. With weekly cashback, every losing week pays out. With monthly cashback, a single big win during the month can wipe out the entire month's eligibility.

Concrete example. A player loses €120 in three weeks and wins €100 in the fourth. Monthly net loss: €260. 10% monthly cashback = €26. With weekly 10% cashback, three losing weeks pay €12 each (€36 total) and the winning week pays €0. Weekly outperforms monthly by **€10 on a single month** — and the gap widens with higher variance.

**Always prefer weekly payout** over monthly at the same percentage. Even an 8% weekly cashback usually beats a 10% monthly cashback across a typical playing pattern.

## The Hidden Maximum Cap Problem

The most common pitfall in cashback comparisons: the **maximum payout cap**, often set in the operator's terms but rarely highlighted in promotional pages.

Typical patterns:

- "10% cashback **up to €100 per week**" — caps at €100 regardless of actual losses. A player losing €2,000 in a week gets €100, not €200.
- "10% cashback **up to 10x deposit**" — caps tied to recent deposit volume rather than loss volume
- "10% cashback **excluding live dealer**" — gameplay restriction that reduces the eligible loss base

A "10% cashback" with a €100 weekly cap is functionally a 10% cashback only for players losing under €1,000/week. Above that threshold, the effective rate drops linearly toward zero. For €500/week recreational players, the cap rarely matters. For €2,000/week high-rollers, the cap determines everything.

**Before claiming a cashback offer, always check:**
1. Maximum weekly/monthly payout in absolute Euro terms
2. Minimum loss threshold (some operators require €50+ net loss before cashback applies)
3. Game eligibility (slots only? Live dealer? Progressive jackpots?)
4. Wagering on the cashback amount (1x or 0x preferred; 5x+ defeats the value)

## FAQ

**What does 10% cashback mean in casino terms?**

10% cashback returns 10% of your **net losses** over a defined period (usually weekly or monthly) as withdrawable cash. Net losses = deposits minus withdrawals during the period. The cashback is typically credited with 1x wagering or no wagering at all.
